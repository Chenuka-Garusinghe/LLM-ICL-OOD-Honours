\section{Purpose, scope and how to read this document}
%======================================================================

This document specifies the complete synthetic experiment. It covers how
tasks and data are generated, how rows are presented to the language model
(feature naming, serialisation, prompts), how demonstrations are selected,
how the model is queried, how results are evaluated, how SATA is trained,
and how the model's internal computation is probed. Every design choice is
stated with its rationale. Where a property can be proved (label directions,
base rates, invariances, freedom from leakage), the proof is given; where a
property is only checked empirically, the check and its threshold are given.

The companion document \texttt{docs/research\_plan.md} gives the phase
schedule (P0--P7), gates and reading list. Section numbers there and here are
independent; phases are referred to as P0--P7 in both.
Section~\ref{sec:attribution} lists the published work that each part of the
design draws on; citations in the text give the details.

\paragraph{Version history.}
v2 (22 September 2026) documented the pipeline as then implemented,
including several defects that invalidate its results; they are listed in
Part~\ref{part:issues}. v3 (this version) specifies the redesigned
experiment for 7--8B models, run in PyTorch on Apple's Metal (MPS) backend.
The 27 September revision adds the sources of each design element
(Section~\ref{sec:attribution}) and a bibliography, and drops the MLX
fallback: PyTorch is the only backend. The 28 September revision records the P1
implementation: status labels, the refinements the implementation made to
the mechanisms and seeding, the rendered prompt, the results schema and the
G1 results.

\section{Research questions and hypotheses}
\label{sec:rqs}
%======================================================================
\status{Planned (P4--P6)}

In-context learning \citep{brown2020language,dong2024survey} adapts a
language model to a task from demonstrations in its prompt.
\citet{wei2023larger} found that the ability to follow in-context evidence
over semantic priors emerges with model scale. This thesis asks whether
demonstration selection, and in particular a learned selector (SATA) trained
with signals taken from the model itself, can help 7--8B models do so under
controlled distribution shift. Evidence so far suggests 7--8B models follow
contradicting demonstrations only partially: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct agrees
with fully flipped sentiment labels about half the time
\citep{krishnakumar2025semantic}, and with flipped labels in-context
accuracy plateaus below its level with the original labels
\citep{kossen2024incontext}. Models of this size also contain machinery for
following the context: under false demonstrations their later layers switch
to the demonstrated labels \citep{halawi2024overthinking}, and a single
direction in the residual stream controls whether they follow the context
or their prior \citep{minder2025controllable}. The framing is therefore a
graded contest between prior and demonstrations, in the spirit of Bayesian
accounts of in-context learning \citep{xie2022explanation},
rather than an absent ability.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{L{1.1cm}L{6.6cm}L{7.6cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{RQ} & \textbf{Question} & \textbf{Hypothesis} \\
\midrule
RQ1 & Does in-context learning degrade out of distribution? &
H1: calibrated balanced accuracy with random demonstrations is lower on
covariate-shift, spurious-reversal and concept-shift (flipped-name) queries
than on ID queries. \\
RQ2 & Do different selection strategies win under different shift types? &
H2: strategy $\times$ shift interaction; counter-spurious helps most under
spurious reversal, coverage/kNN under covariate shift, counter-prior under
concept shift. \\
RQ3 & Does the model rely on the correct features, and why does the prior
win when it does? &
H3: under flipped names, behavioural reliance follows the demonstrations
less than under abstract names while self-reports follow the names; the
logit lens shows a layer where prior and demonstration evidence diverge. \\
RQ4 & Does SATA beat hand-designed strategies, and do model-informed
signals help? &
H4: SATA-v2 is at least as good as the best hand-designed strategy on
worst-environment accuracy; SATA-MI beats SATA-v2 and the SATA-names control
under flipped names. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Research question &
Following in-context evidence over semantic priors emerges with scale
\citep{wei2023larger}. 7--8B models follow contradicting demonstrations

\section{Feature roles and column-role permutation}
\label{sec:roles}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1)}

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[

    noise/.style={feat, fill=yellow!10},

  \foreach \i in {0,...,7} {
    \node[feat] (f\i) at (\i*1.1, 0) {$f_{\i}$};
  }

  \node[noise] (f9) at (9*1.1, 0) {$f_9$};

  \draw[decorate, decoration={brace, amplitude=5pt, raise=3pt}]
    (f0.north west) -- (f7.north east)
    node[midway, above=10pt, font=\footnotesize]
    {3 load-bearing (task-specific) + 5 distractors};

  \node[below=0.4cm of f8, font=\scriptsize\itshape, text=red!70!black] {spurious};
  \node[below=0.4cm of f9, font=\scriptsize\itshape, text=olive] {noise};
  \node[font=\scriptsize\itshape, text=gray] at (5.0,-1.3)
    {generator indices; the displayed column order is permuted per task};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

Inside the generator, load-bearing features are drawn from indices
$\{0,\ldots,7\}$, the spurious feature is index 8 and the noise feature is
index 9. When a task is written to disk, its 10 columns are reordered by a
permutation $\pi_t$ drawn uniformly per task and recorded in the manifest.
Every downstream component reads column roles from the manifest, never from
position.

\begin{lemma}[No positional shortcut]
If $\pi_t$ is uniform on the permutations of $\{0,\ldots,9\}$ and independent
of the task's rule, the displayed position $\pi_t(8)$ of the spurious feature
is uniform on $\{0,\ldots,9\}$ and independent of the rule. A selector
trained across tasks (SATA) therefore cannot identify the spurious column by
its position.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For any position $p$, the number of permutations with $\pi(8)=p$ is $9!$ for
every $p$, so $P(\pi_t(8)=p) = 9!/10! = 1/10$. Independence holds because
$\pi_t$ is drawn independently of the rule.
\end{proof}

In v2 the spurious feature was always $f_8$ (the ninth column), which a
selector trained across tasks can learn by position: a shortcut in the sense
of \citet{geirhos2020shortcut}.

\section{Task sampling}
\label{sec:sample-tasks}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1)}

Sampling tasks from known function classes follows studies of in-context
learning on synthetic functions \citep{garg2022what} and the synthetic
priors used to train tabular foundation models
\citep{muller2022transformers,hollmann2023tabpfn,hollmann2025accurate}.
Synthetic tasks also
avoid the memorisation of public tabular datasets documented for large
language models such as GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 \citep{bordt2024elephants}, and
they provide the ground truth (load-bearing
features, directions and spurious strength) that the analyses need.

Each task $i$ of a suite $S$ is sampled by \texttt{sample\_eval\_tasks}
from its own stream (a NumPy \texttt{SeedSequence} of the task seed, the
suite name and $i$), so its parameters do not depend on the other tasks,
except its family and its spurious direction, which are balanced over the
suite:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Rule family:} fixed by position (the first half of the suite
        linear, the rest tree: tasks 0--11 and 12--23 in \texttt{eval}),
        crossed with domain, which alternates (loan, medical), so each family
        has 6 tasks per domain (Section~\ref{sec:naming}).
  \item \textbf{Load-bearing features:} 3 distinct indices from
        $\{0,\ldots,7\}$, sorted.
  \item \textbf{Linear coefficients:} $c_j = u_j r_j$ with
        $u_j \sim \text{U}(1,2)$ and $r_j$ a Rademacher sign, so every
        load-bearing feature has a non-negligible effect and a definite
        direction.
  \item \textbf{Tree leaf table:} the signed majority with literal signs
        $r_j$, which is uniform over the 8 admissible tables
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:tree}); the code asserts that each table is
        complement antisymmetric and not degenerate.
  \item \textbf{Spurious strength:} $s \sim \text{U}(0.80, 0.90)$.
  \item \textbf{Spurious direction:} $\sigma_t \in \{+1, -1\}$
        (Section~\ref{sec:spurious}), $+1$ for half of the tasks in each
        (family, domain) cell and $-1$ for the other half, in random order,
        from a separate stream so the other draws are unchanged. An odd
        cell's extra sign alternates between cells, so each pilot family (two
        cells of 3 tasks) is still split 3/3.
  \item \textbf{Data seed:} one integer from the same stream, from which
        every split of the task derives (Section~\ref{sec:datasets}).
\end{enumerate}
Two suites are written: \texttt{eval} (24 tasks) and \texttt{pilot}
(12 tasks), from the same distribution with disjoint task ids. The pilot
suite is used only in P2, so the number of demonstrations is chosen without
touching the evaluation tasks. SATA training and validation tasks will be
drawn from the same distribution with disjoint ids (P5).

\section{Rule families}
\label{sec:rules}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1): evaluation uses linear and tree only}

Given $X \in \RR^{n \times 10}$ and optional $u \in [0,1]^n$, the generator
computes $y_{\mathrm{clean}} \in \{0,1\}^n$.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Linear}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

\[
  p_i = \sigmoid\!\left(\mathbf{c}^\top \mathbf{X}_{i,C}\right),
  \qquad
  y_{\mathrm{clean},i} =
  \begin{cases}
    \1[u_i < p_i] & \text{data generation (Bernoulli)} \\
    \1[p_i > 0.5] & \text{probes (MAP)}
  \end{cases}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Monotone directions]
\label{lem:linear-direction}
$P(y_{\mathrm{clean}}=1 \mid x) = \sigmoid(\mathbf{c}^\top x_C)$ is strictly
increasing in $x_j$ when $c_j > 0$ and strictly decreasing when $c_j < 0$,
for every value of the other features. The direction of feature $j$ is
therefore well defined: $s_j = \sign(c_j)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\partial p / \partial x_j = \sigmoid'(\mathbf{c}^\top x_C)\, c_j$ and
$\sigmoid' > 0$ everywhere.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Reliability of the causal signal.}
With $|C| = 3$ and $|c_j| \sim \text{U}(1,2)$, the Bayes accuracy (the
accuracy of the Bayes classifier; \citealp{devroye1996probabilistic}) from the
load-bearing features, $\E[\max(p, 1-p)]$, has mean $0.817$ (5th--95th
percentile $0.788$--$0.840$ over tasks, by simulation). This is comparable to
the spurious strength $s \in [0.80, 0.90]$, so in linear tasks the spurious
feature is about as reliable as the causal signal: the shortcut competes.
The design follows Colored MNIST \citep{arjovsky2019invariant}, where
flipping 25\% of the labels caps the causal (digit-shape) signal at 75\%
accuracy while colour agrees with the label 80--90\% of the time in training
and 10\% at test. Labels are therefore drawn from $p_i$ rather than
thresholded: a noise-free linear rule would be perfectly reliable and would
dominate the shortcut.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Tree (signed majority)}
\label{sec:tree}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

With $k = 3$ load-bearing features $C_1, C_2, C_3$ and bits
$b_j = \1[X_{i,C_j} > 0]$, the leaf index is
$L = b_1 + 2b_2 + 4b_3$ and $y_{\mathrm{clean}} = \text{leaf\_labels}[L]$.
Splitting at zero follows the decision-tree class of \citet{garg2022what},
whose depth-4 trees send an input left or right by the sign of a randomly
chosen coordinate; here the tree has depth 3 with one feature per level,
and its binary leaf labels are constrained as follows.
Leaf tables are sampled by \texttt{\_sample\_tree\_leaf\_labels}, which
imposes complement antisymmetry, $\text{leaf\_labels}[L] =
1 - \text{leaf\_labels}[7-L]$, and rejects dictator and parity functions.

\begin{theorem}[Admissible trees are signed majorities]
\label{thm:tree}
For $k=3$, the leaf tables accepted by \texttt{\_sample\_tree\_leaf\_labels}
are exactly the 8 functions
\[
  y_{\mathrm{clean}} = \Maj(\ell_1, \ell_2, \ell_3), \qquad
  \ell_j \in \{b_j,\ 1 - b_j\}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Complement antisymmetry says $f(\bar b) = 1 - f(b)$, where $\bar b$ flips
all three bits: $f$ is \emph{self-dual} (an odd function in the $\pm 1$
notation of \citealp{odonnell2014analysis}, which also defines the dictator,
parity and majority functions used below). The 8 inputs form 4 complementary
pairs $\{b, \bar b\}$, and a self-dual function is determined by a free
choice of its value on one member of each pair, so there are exactly
$2^4 = 16$ self-dual functions of 3 bits.

The sampler rejects the 6 dictators $b_j$ and $1-b_j$ (each self-dual) and
the 2 parities $b_1 \oplus b_2 \oplus b_3$ and its complement (self-dual,
because flipping three bits flips the parity). That leaves $16 - 8 = 8$
functions.

Each signed majority is self-dual, since
$\Maj(1-\ell_1, 1-\ell_2, 1-\ell_3) = 1 - \Maj(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)$. It
depends on all three bits, so it is not a dictator. It is monotone in each
literal while parity is not, so it is not a parity. There are $2^3 = 8$ sign
patterns, giving 8 distinct functions (distinctness is checked by exhaustive
enumeration in Appendix~\ref{app:tree}). These 8 functions lie in the
admissible set of size 8, so they are the whole set.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Tree directions]
Every admissible tree task is monotone in the sign of each load-bearing
feature, with direction $s_j = +1$ if $\ell_j = b_j$ and $s_j = -1$ if
$\ell_j = 1 - b_j$. Tree rules are deterministic, so the Bayes accuracy from
the load-bearing features is $1 - \eta = 0.98 > s$: in tree tasks the causal
rule dominates the shortcut. Linear and tree tasks therefore contrast a
competitive shortcut with a dominated one.
\end{corollary}
A dominated shortcut can still be adopted:
\citet{nagarajan2021understanding} show that gradient-trained classifiers
rely on a spurious feature more as its correlation grows, even when the
invariant features predict the label perfectly. Tree tasks therefore still
test reliance on $f_8$, with a weaker incentive.

\paragraph{Regime.} \texttt{\_regime} encodes the sign pattern of the
load-bearing features (\texttt{load\_bearing\_features()}: all causal
features for linear tasks, the first 3 for tree tasks) with the leaf-index
encoding $b_1 + 2b_2 + 4b_3$. For a tree task it therefore equals the leaf
(tested); for linear tasks it is the orthant, used only for stratification.
(In v2 it used all 3--5 causal features while \texttt{tree} used only the
first 3, splitting each true leaf.)

\paragraph{Directions.} \texttt{directions()} returns $s_j$ for each
load-bearing feature: $\sign(c_j)$ for linear tasks, and for tree tasks the
sign of the label difference between $b_j = 1$ and $b_j = 0$ over the leaf
table, which recovers the literal signs of the signed majority. A test nudges
every load-bearing feature by $+s_j$ and checks that the share of rows with
the MAP label 1 rises.

\subsection{Families excluded from evaluation}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{lemma}[Threshold confounds naming]
The threshold rule $y = \1[\sum_{j} \1[x_{C_j} > t_j] \geq 2]$ is
non-decreasing in every load-bearing feature, so $s_j = +1$ for all $j$.
\end{lemma}
Under the naming protocol (Section~\ref{sec:naming}), aligned threshold
tasks would always carry positive-association names and flipped ones
negative-association names, so the naming condition would be confounded with
name valence. Sparse interaction, $y = \1[x_i x_j > \theta]$, is not monotone
in $x_i$ (the sign of its effect depends on $x_j$), so no direction exists.
Both remain in the generator but are not evaluated.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\texttt{covariate}}{covariate} --- \texorpdfstring{$P(X)$}{P(X)} shift}
\label{sec:covariate}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Covariate shift changes $P(X)$ while the labelling rule $P(y \mid x)$ stays
fixed \citep{shimodaira2000improving,morenotorres2012unifying}. Transformers
trained to learn linear functions in context are brittle under it
\citep{zhang2024trained}, which motivates testing it here. The shift should
move the rule's own inputs, so that queries fall where the demonstrations
are sparse, without changing the label rate: a change of $P(y)$ would add
prior-probability (label) shift \citep{morenotorres2012unifying}.

The shift vector moves three features: two load-bearing features in
opposite label directions and one distractor. Draw two distinct
load-bearing features $i, j$ at random, with directions $s_i, s_j$, and a
distractor $d$ from the features in $\{0,\ldots,7\}$ outside $C$ (features 8
and 9 are overwritten in step 4, so shifting them would do nothing). With a push
$m$,
\[
  \delta_i = s_i\, m / |c_i|, \qquad
  \delta_j = -s_j\, m / |c_j|, \qquad
  \delta_d \sim \text{U}(1,2) \times \text{Rademacher}.
\]
For linear tasks $m \sim \text{U}\big(\max(|c_i|,|c_j|),\ 2\min(|c_i|,|c_j|)\big)$,
which puts both $|\delta_i|$ and $|\delta_j|$ in $[1, 2]$ (the interval is
never empty, because $|c| \in [1,2]$). Tree rules read only signs, so
$|c_i| = |c_j| = 1$ and $m \sim \text{U}(1,2)$. Every shifted feature
therefore moves by 1--2 standard deviations: feature $i$ towards label 1,
feature $j$ towards label 0, and the distractor up or down at random.

\begin{lemma}[The label rate is unchanged]
\label{lem:covariate-neutral}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
  \item Linear: $\mathbf{c}^\top(x + \delta) = \mathbf{c}^\top x$ for every
        row, so every row's label distribution, and hence $P(y=1)$, is
        unchanged.
  \item Tree: $P(y_{\mathrm{clean}} = 1)$ stays $1/2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) $c_i\delta_i = s_i|c_i| \cdot s_i m/|c_i| = m$ and $c_j\delta_j = -m$,
and $d \notin C$ contributes nothing. (b) Let $v_k$ be feature $k$'s vote,
$\1[s_k x_k > 0]$ (Section~\ref{sec:tree}); the votes are independent, and
each is 1 with probability $1/2$ before the shift. After it,
$s_i(x_i + s_i m) > 0$ with probability $p_i = \Phi(m)$ and
$s_j(x_j - s_j m) > 0$ with probability $p_j = 1 - \Phi(m)$; the third vote
is unchanged. The majority is 1 when both shifted votes are 1, or exactly
one of them is and the third vote is 1:
$p_i p_j + \tfrac12[p_i(1-p_j) + (1-p_i)p_j] = \tfrac12(p_i + p_j) = \tfrac12$.
\end{proof}

The manifest records each shifted feature's role (\texttt{towards\_1},
\texttt{towards\_0}, \texttt{distractor}), the push $m$, and a check (not a
filter): the MAP label-1 rate of the same 20{,}000 fresh rows before and
after the shift. Over the 24 evaluation tasks the difference is exactly 0
for the linear tasks and at most 0.009 for the tree tasks.

\paragraph{Why not rejection sampling.} Until 30 September 2026 the shift
moved 3 features drawn at random from $\{0,\ldots,7\}$ and was redrawn (up
to 50 times) until the label-1 rate, estimated on a 2048-row probe, was
within 0.03 of the ID rate. That filter selects distractors: a shift of
distractors cannot change any label and always passed, while a shift of
exactly one load-bearing feature moved the rate by a median of 0.23 and
never passed. In the evaluation suite 21 of 24 tasks shifted distractors
only, so for most tasks the environment tested sensitivity to irrelevant
features rather than a shift of the rule's inputs. The rejection sampler is
kept only for the families that are not evaluated (threshold, sparse
interaction). The redesign changed only the covariate rows: the pool, the
ID and spurious-reversal rows and every query id outside the covariate
environment are identical (checked file by file).


%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Covariate probes (exploratory, added after P4)}
\label{sec:covariate-probes}

The pre-registered covariate contrast C1a found no harm (AUROC $+0.024$).
Two properties of the \texttt{covariate} design explain much of this
(\texttt{hiccups/18}). Every covariate row receives the same shift vector, so
for a scorer roughly linear in the features the shift cannot reorder rows
within the environment, which is what within-environment AUROC measures; and
$f_8$ is regenerated from the label in every environment, so the shortcut
stays valid. Qwen's scores do move: for the same prompt, covariate queries
score 0.44 to 1.21 logits higher than ID queries (with demonstrations only),
and ranking ID positives against covariate negatives gives AUROC 0.46--0.54
under abstract names. Three probe splits, appended after the standard ones
so that every existing row, value and query id is unchanged, test the
covariate question further:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{covariate\_scale}: every covariate except $f_8$ is
        multiplied by its own factor $a_j \sim \mathcal{U}(1.5, 3)$, fixed
        per task, before the rule is applied. Each row moves in proportion to
        its own values, so rows can be reordered for a scorer whose weights
        differ from the rule's, and values reach about 6 SD. $P(y \mid x)$ is
        unchanged, the label rate stays $1/2$ (every feature is symmetric),
        and $f_8$ is generated from the labels as usual.
  \item \texttt{<env>\_f8neutral} for \texttt{id}, \texttt{covariate} and
        \texttt{covariate\_scale}: the same queries with $f_8$ replaced by
        pool $f_8$ values drawn at random, so it no longer carries the label;
        query ids match the source, so the pairs are matched query by query.
\end{itemize}
On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (24 tasks, 3 seeds), the variance shift lowers AUROC
only under flipped names (by 0.09--0.13; intervals above 0 for random,
label\_diversity and rule\_diversity), where stretching the rule features
amplifies a reading of them in the wrong direction. Making $f_8$
uninformative lowers ID AUROC by 0.05--0.08 (not under counter\_spurious
demonstrations, which never teach it), but does not widen the covariate gap
(about 0), so shortcut reliance does not hide covariate harm here. These
contrasts are exploratory and not part of the frozen family
(\texttt{EXPLORATORY\_CONTRASTS} in \texttt{src/evaluation/analysis.py}).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\texttt{spurious\_reversal}}{spurious-reversal}}
\label{sec:spurious-reversal}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------
$s' = 1 - s$; the direction $\sigma_t$ and everything else are unchanged.
Sign agreement between $\sigma_t f_8$ and the label falls from $s$ to $1-s$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:agreement}). This
mirrors the test environment of Colored MNIST \citep{arjovsky2019invariant},
where colour--label agreement falls from 80--90\% in training to 10\% while
the causal mechanism is unchanged. \citet{chen2026incontext} use the same
parameters to test transformers trained for in-context learning and find
that they fail to adapt when the correlation reverses.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\texttt{mechanism}}{mechanism} --- why it is a placebo for in-context learning}
\label{sec:mechanism}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The rule is applied to $\tilde X$ with $\tilde X_{:,C} = -X_{:,C}$, and for
linear tasks the coefficients are rescaled, $c'_j = c_j v_j$ with
$v_j \sim \text{U}(0.5, 1.5)$. The model sees the original $X$.

\begin{proposition}[The mechanism shift inverts the ID rule]
\label{prop:mechanism}
Let $y_{\mathrm{id}}(x)$ and $y_{\mathrm{mech}}(x)$ be the clean labels of the
same task in the two environments.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
  \item Tree: $y_{\mathrm{mech}}(x) = 1 - y_{\mathrm{id}}(x)$ for almost every $x$.
  \item Linear: $P(y_{\mathrm{mech}} = 1 \mid x) = 1 - \sigmoid(\mathbf{c}'^\top x_C)$,
        where $\mathbf{c}'$ has the same signs as $\mathbf{c}$. The Bayes
        decision of the mechanism environment, $\1[\mathbf{c}'^\top x_C < 0]$,
        is the negation of the ID Bayes decision, $\1[\mathbf{c}^\top x_C > 0]$,
        everywhere except on the set where $\sign(\mathbf{c}'^\top x_C) \neq
        \sign(\mathbf{c}^\top x_C)$, a region next to the ID decision
        boundary.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) Negating the load-bearing features maps each bit to
$1[-x_j > 0] = 1 - b_j$ (except on the null set $x_j = 0$), so the leaf
index becomes $7 - L$, and complement antisymmetry gives
$\text{leaf\_labels}[7-L] = 1 - \text{leaf\_labels}[L]$.
(b) $P(y_{\mathrm{mech}}=1\mid x) = \sigmoid(\mathbf{c}'^\top(-x_C)) =
1 - \sigmoid(\mathbf{c}'^\top x_C)$ because $\sigmoid(-z) = 1 - \sigmoid(z)$.
The mechanism Bayes decision is therefore $\1[\mathbf{c}'^\top x_C < 0]$.
Since $v_j > 0$, $\mathbf{c}'$ has the signs of $\mathbf{c}$, and wherever
$\sign(\mathbf{c}'^\top x_C) = \sign(\mathbf{c}^\top x_C)$ this equals
$1 - \1[\mathbf{c}^\top x_C > 0]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Consequences]
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
  \item \emph{Demonstrations from the ID pool.} A model that recovers the ID
        rule from ID demonstrations is systematically wrong on mechanism
        queries (accuracy about $1 - \mathrm{acc}_{\mathrm{ID}}$). Only $f_8$,
        generated from the post-shift label, keeps its relation to the
        label, so above-chance accuracy indicates spurious reliance rather
        than generalisation.
  \item \emph{Demonstrations from the mechanism environment.} $X$ is
        symmetric ($X \stackrel{d}{=} -X$), so the mechanism environment is
        distributed as another task of the same family with its signs
        flipped. With matched demonstrations it is just a new ID task, and
        ID $\approx$ OOD by construction: a placebo.
  \item \emph{Naming.} If feature names are aligned with the ID rule, the
        mechanism environment with matched demonstrations is the ID
        environment with flipped names, up to the coefficient rescale
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:concept-naming}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

Changing the rule while $P(X)$ stays fixed is concept shift
\citep{morenotorres2012unifying}; in causal terms it replaces one mechanism
and leaves the others unchanged \citep{peters2017elements}. On real tabular
data most large performance drops are dominated by such $Y \mid X$ shifts
\citep{liu2023need}, so concept shift needs a valid operationalisation.
It is therefore realised through naming (Section~\ref{sec:naming}), an
input-side analogue of the flipped labels of \citet{wei2023larger}. The
mechanism environment is kept for SATA
training, where it is always paired with matched demonstrations, as on
\texttt{main}.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\texttt{extrapolation}}{extrapolation} and \texorpdfstring{\texttt{missing\_feature}}{missing-feature}}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------
Extrapolation replaces 2--3 load-bearing features per row with
$\pm\,\text{U}(2,4)$; missing feature redraws the first load-bearing feature
independently of the label. Both are retained in the generator and not
evaluated.

\section{Spurious feature construction (\texorpdfstring{$f_8$}{f8})}
\label{sec:spurious}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1); per-task direction added in P2}

\[
  d = \Phi^{-1}(s), \qquad
  X_{i,8} = \sigma_t \big( (2\,y_{\mathrm{clean},i} - 1)\, d + \varepsilon_i \big),
  \quad \varepsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1),
\]
where $\sigma_t \in \{+1, -1\}$ is the task's spurious direction
(Section~\ref{sec:sample-tasks}). So $X_8 \mid y{=}1 \sim \mathcal{N}(\sigma_t d,1)$
and $X_8 \mid y{=}0 \sim \mathcal{N}(-\sigma_t d,1)$: in half of the tasks a
large $f_8$ indicates label 1, in the other half label 0. This is a class-conditional Gaussian
feature, like the environmental features in the data model of
\citet{rosenfeld2021risks}, whose class-conditional mean and variance change
across environments; here only the sign of the mean changes (spurious
reversal). The range of $s$ matches the colour--label agreement in the
training environments of Colored MNIST \citep{arjovsky2019invariant}.

\begin{proposition}[Sign agreement]
\label{prop:agreement}
$P\big(\sign(\sigma_t X_8) = \sign(2y_{\mathrm{clean}}-1)\big) = s$; under
spurious reversal it is $1-s$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\sigma_t X_8 = (2y_{\mathrm{clean}}-1)d + \varepsilon$. For $y=1$:
$P(\sigma_t X_8 > 0) = P(Z > -d) = \Phi(d) = s$. For $y=0$:
$P(\sigma_t X_8 < 0) = P(Z < d) = s$. Under reversal
$d' = \Phi^{-1}(1-s) = -d$, so the same computation gives $1-s$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why the direction is random.} In v2 and in the first P2 run,
$\sigma_t = +1$ for every task. With abstract names, the zero-shot margin of
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on the pilot tasks rose with every feature value: its
Spearman correlation with the plain sum of the 10 features was $0.70$, and
82\% of the per-task least-squares slopes of the margin on the features were
positive. The load-bearing directions are random, so such a prior helps in
some tasks and hurts in others; the spurious direction was not, so the prior
backed the shortcut in every task. Scoring the ID queries by the sum of the
features gives AUROC $0.628$, and $0.511$ once $f_8$ is left out; Qwen's
zero-shot AUROC was $0.633$, so its zero-shot signal came from $f_8$ through
the prior. Under spurious reversal the same prior would have lowered every
condition, zero-shot included, confounding demonstration-induced shortcut
use with a prior shared by all strategies. With $\sigma_t$ balanced, the
prior backs the shortcut in half of the tasks and opposes it in the other
half, and the two halves can be compared (prior-aligned against
prior-opposed shortcut).

Because $\sigma_t$ multiplies the whole column, $\sigma_t = -1$ changes
nothing but the sign of $f_8$: the other columns, the labels, $|f_8|$, the
agreement flags and therefore the counter-spurious demonstration sets are
identical to those of $\sigma_t = +1$ (tested). The archived first P2 run
and the rerun therefore differ only in the sign of $f_8$ on the 6 pilot
tasks with $\sigma_t = -1$.

\begin{proposition}[Scale of $f_8$]
With balanced classes, $\E X_8 = 0$ and $\Var X_8 = 1 + d^2$. For
$s = 0.80, 0.85, 0.90$ the standard deviation is $1.307$, $1.440$ and
$1.626$, against 1 for every other feature.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$X_8$ is an equal mixture of $\mathcal{N}(\pm d, 1)$; its mean is 0 and its
second moment is $1 + d^2$.
\end{proof}

Unstandardised, $f_8$ is visibly wider than the other columns (a v2 defect).
v3 z-scores every feature with the task's ID-pool mean and standard
deviation.

\begin{lemma}[What standardisation preserves]
\label{lem:standardise}
Z-scoring is an affine map $x \mapsto (x - \hat\mu)/\hat\sigma$ with
$\hat\sigma > 0$, so the Pearson correlation of $f_8$ with any variable is
unchanged. Sign agreement is not preserved: centring $\sigma_t X_8$ at its
pool mean $\hat\mu_8 = (2\hat\pi - 1)d$, where $\hat\pi$ is the pool label
rate, changes agreement to
$\hat\pi\,\Phi(d - \hat\mu_8) + (1 - \hat\pi)\,\Phi(d + \hat\mu_8)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\corr(aX + b, Y) = \corr(X, Y)$ for $a > 0$. For the agreement, the
displayed sign, read in the task's direction, is
$\sign(\sigma_t X_8 - \hat\mu_8)$; condition on $y$ and use the normal CDF.
\end{proof}

At $s = 0.85$ and $\hat\pi \in \{0.35, 0.65\}$, agreement after centring is
$0.817$ against $0.850$. Therefore every statistic about $f_8$ reliance is
defined on the generator scale, and correlation-based statistics are
preferred to sign-based ones.

\section{Label noise and row metadata}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1)}

With probability $\eta = 0.02$ the label is flipped: $y = 1 - y_{\mathrm{clean}}$
(random classification noise; \citealp{angluin1988learning}).
$f_8$ is generated from $y_{\mathrm{clean}}$, so about 2\% of rows have $f_8$
misaligned with the label the model sees. Each row stores:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Definition} \\
\midrule
\texttt{y\_clean}, \texttt{label} & rule label; label after noise (shown in prompts) \\
\texttt{regime} & sign pattern of the load-bearing features \\
\texttt{agree\_clean} & $\1[\sign(\sigma_t X_8) = \sign(2y_{\mathrm{clean}}-1)]$ (generator scale) \\
\texttt{agree\_obs} & $\1[\sign(\sigma_t X_8) = \sign(2y-1)]$; used by counter-spurious selection \\
\texttt{spurious\_raw} & $X_8$ on the generator scale, sign included (for magnitude matching) \\
\texttt{query\_id} & 0--19 for the environment's queries, $-1$ otherwise \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

v2's \texttt{is\_counter\_spurious} used $y_{\mathrm{clean}}$; it now follows
the observed label (\texttt{is\_counter\_spurious} $= \lnot$
\texttt{agree\_obs}), because that is what the demonstrations show. The
generator check of the spurious strength uses \texttt{agree\_clean}, which
the construction sets to $s$ exactly.

\section{Naming protocol}
\label{sec:naming}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P3): \texttt{src/data/naming.py}, \texttt{configs/lexicons.yaml}}

The naming factor carries the label manipulations of \citet{wei2023larger}
over to feature names. Abstract names play the role of their semantically
unrelated labels, removing the prior; flipped names play the role of their
flipped labels, making the prior contradict the rule.
\citet{nafar2025learning} compare named and anonymised features in
in-context regression and find that names let models draw on prior
knowledge in addition to learning from the examples, and
\citet{pan2023what} separate task recognition, which relies on such priors,
from task learning. Feature names matter for fine-tuned models too: correct
names help and shuffled names hurt \citep{dinh2022lift}. Language models'
knowledge of variable names can also serve directly as a prior
\citep{choi2022lmpriors}.

\begin{definition}[Lexicon]
A domain $D$ has a label meaning ($1$ and $0$), a set of antonym pairs
$\mathcal{P}_D = \{(p_m, q_m)\}$, where $p_m$ is a name whose real-world
association with label 1 is positive and $q_m$ one whose association is
negative, and a set $\mathcal{N}_D$ of plausible names with weak priors. Two
domains are used: loan approval (1 = approved) and medical risk
(1 = high risk). Domain is a between-task factor. The candidate and final
lexicons are in Appendix~\ref{app:lexicon}; the final names come from the P3
screening (Section~\ref{sec:priors}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Namings]
For task $t$ with directions $s_j$ ($j \in C$), assign each load-bearing
feature a distinct pair $m(j)$ at random (seeded). Then:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{abstract}: every column is named by its displayed position,
        \texttt{f0}--\texttt{f9};
  \item \textbf{aligned}: feature $j$ is named $p_{m(j)}$ if $s_j = +1$ and
        $q_{m(j)}$ if $s_j = -1$;
  \item \textbf{flipped}: the other member of the pair;
  \item in aligned and flipped, every other column (distractors, $f_8$,
        $f_9$) gets a distinct name from $\mathcal{N}_D$, identical in the
        two conditions.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

Directions come from ground truth: $s_j = \sign(c_j)$ for linear tasks
(Lemma~\ref{lem:linear-direction}) and the literal signs for tree tasks
(Theorem~\ref{thm:tree}). The pool correlation of each feature with the label
is computed only as a check. A task's pairs and weak names are drawn from
a stream seeded by the task alone (\texttt{naming\_seed}), so the aligned
and flipped namings share them.

The design intended pairs whose members have opposite measured prior slopes
of similar magnitude, so that flipping changes the direction of the prior but
not its strength. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct's priors are not symmetric
(Section~\ref{sec:priors}): a numeric prior makes almost every slope
positive, and names that should lower $P(1)$ mostly lower the slope without
reversing it. The final pairs are therefore those with the largest measured
contrast $\beta_p - \beta_q$. Flipping then weakens the prior on
positive-direction features and turns it against the rule on
negative-direction features, where a positive name such as
\emph{credit score} sits on a column whose larger values lower the label. The
effect is strong in the loan domain and weak in the medical domain.

\begin{proposition}[Identification]
\label{prop:identification}
For a fixed task, demonstration set and query, the aligned and flipped
prompts differ only in the names of the three load-bearing columns. The
numbers, labels, column order and system message are identical. For any
predictor whose output does not depend on display names, expected accuracy
is identical under aligned and flipped naming, so a difference in the
model's accuracy between the two is attributable to the semantics of the
names.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By construction of the namings and the prompt template
(Section~\ref{sec:prompts}); the demonstration sets of naming-blind
selectors are identical across namings (Section~\ref{sec:seeding}).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Concept shift through naming]
\label{prop:concept-naming}
Let names be aligned with the ID rule. With demonstrations drawn from the
same environment, the mechanism environment presents the model with a rule
whose directions are the negations of the names' directions, which is
exactly the flipped condition on ID data (up to the linear coefficient
rescale).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:mechanism}, the mechanism rule has directions
$-s_j$. Aligned names encode $+s_j$, so names and rule disagree on every
load-bearing feature, which is the definition of the flipped condition.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What the names reveal.}
In named conditions the load-bearing columns carry strong-prior names and
the others weak-prior names, so name salience correlates with causal
relevance by design: the experiment manipulates the \emph{direction} of the
prior, not which features it points to. The LLM and SATA-MI can both use
this; the SATA-names control (Section~\ref{sec:sata-controls}) receives the
same information, so comparisons between them are fair. An optional
robustness check gives one distractor a strong-prior name.

\section{Serialisation}
\label{sec:serialisation}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1)}

Serialising rows as text for a language model follows TabLLM
\citep{hegselmann2023tabllm}, which compares nine serialisations including a
list of name--value pairs, and LIFT \citep{dinh2022lift}. The format here is
a compact name--value list. Each row is converted to text by
\texttt{serialise\_row()}:
\begin{lstlisting}
f0: 0.80; f1: -0.45; f2: -0.30; f3: 1.52; f4: -0.91; f5: 1.10; f6: 0.07; f7: -1.33; f8: 0.44; f9: 0.22 -> 1
credit score: 0.80; branch number: -0.45; ... ; debt-to-income ratio: 1.10; ... -> 1
\end{lstlisting}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Format \texttt{name: value; \ldots\ -> label}; query rows end with
        \texttt{->} (instruct models) or \texttt{-> } (base models,
        Section~\ref{sec:prompts}).
  \item Values are z-scores, fixed width with 2 decimals (\verb|f"{v:.2f}"|).
  \item Column order is the displayed order after $\pi_t$, frozen per task
        and identical in every condition. \texttt{ordered\_feature\_names}
        sorts by raw column name, and the raw names are stored in displayed
        order, so renaming never reorders a prompt.
  \item Display names come from the naming's codebook
        (\texttt{name\_extended}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Selection engine}
\label{sec:selection}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1, P3); SATA in P5}

Selection is a \emph{mechanism} (which rows are preferred) crossed with a
\emph{composition} (how many of each label):
\[
  \texttt{select}(\text{mechanism}, \text{composition}, \text{pool},
  \text{query}, k, \text{seed}) \to (\text{pool indices}, \text{metadata}).
\]
\textbf{free}: the mechanism decides the label counts. \textbf{balanced}:
exactly $k/2$ demonstrations per class, the mechanism choosing within each
class.

\begin{proposition}[Label counts are controlled]
\label{prop:balanced}
Under balanced composition every mechanism produces the same demonstration
label multiset, $k/2$ zeros and $k/2$ ones, whenever each class has at
least $k/2$ pool rows. Differences between balanced mechanisms therefore
cannot be caused by label proportions, which are known to drive a model's
output distribution (majority-label bias;
\citealp{zhao2021calibrate,min2022rethinking}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The quota fixes the count per class before the mechanism chooses rows.
With 256 pool rows and a label rate in $[0.35, 0.65]$ (G1 gate), each class
has at least about 90 rows, far more than $k/2 \leq 16$.
\end{proof}

The label, feature-range, rule and counter-spurious strategies are the four
diversity protocols proposed in the project's literature review. Similarity
selection follows the nearest-neighbour retrieval of \citet{liu2022what},
and covering the relevant structure of a task echoes diverse and
coverage-based selection \citep{levy2023diverse,gupta2023coverage}.

%======================================================================
\section{Mechanisms}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1, P3)}

\paragraph{random.} Uniform without replacement.

\paragraph{feature\_coverage.} The 3 pool features with the highest mutual
information with the label are cut into 3 quantile bins, and a row's cell is
its combination of bins (up to 27 cells). Within each class, cells are
visited in random order, one row each, until the quota is filled. Mutual
information is estimated with scikit-learn's nearest-neighbour estimator
\citep{pedregosa2011scikit,kraskov2004estimating,ross2014mutual}. The version
ported from \texttt{main} assigned rows feature by feature: the top feature's
three bins absorbed every row, so the other two features never formed a
stratum. On all 24 evaluation tasks the top-3 features include the spurious
feature (with two rule features in 23 of them, and never the noise feature).
With $s \in [0.80, 0.90]$ it is more predictive than any single rule feature
(its AUROC alone is $\Phi(\sqrt 2\, d) \approx 0.93$,
Section~\ref{sec:spurious}), and the pool's mutual information cannot tell it
apart from a causal one. Covering its bins within each class over-samples the
rows on which it disagrees with the label, so feature\_range's sets show it
agreeing in 0.66 of rows, against 0.84 for label\_diversity
(\texttt{hiccups/19}).

\paragraph{gt\_regime.} Rows are grouped by ground-truth regime; within each
class, regimes are visited in random order, one row each. A row is eligible
for its class only if it is not a label-noise row
($y = y_{\mathrm{clean}}$) and its class is the pool majority in its regime.
Without that filter, the few noise rows sitting in the other class's leaves
would each form a stratum and be over-sampled. Ineligible rows are used only
if the eligible ones run out. For a tree task each class occupies 4 of the 8
regimes, so with $k = 8$ every leaf is represented once, with its own label
(tested on all 12 tree tasks).

\paragraph{feature\_knn.} Euclidean distance on the 10 z-scored columns; the
$k/2$ nearest rows of each class. This is the nearest-neighbour retrieval of
\citet{liu2022what}, in feature space rather than sentence-embedding space. It depends only on numbers, so it selects
the same demonstrations under every naming. It replaces v2's text similarity
with the sentence-transformers model \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}
\citep{reimers2019sentence,wang2020minilm}, whose embeddings would change
with the display names and confound the naming factor.

\paragraph{counter\_prior.} Score
$\1[\sign \tilde m_0(x_i; N) \neq 2y_i - 1]\cdot\1[y_i = y_{\mathrm{clean},i}]$,
where $\tilde m_0$ is the model's measured zero-shot margin on pool row $i$
under the unit's naming, centred on the pool median
(Section~\ref{sec:priors}): rows whose label contradicts the model's prior
\emph{and} follows the rule (so label-noise rows are not rewarded). Rows
scoring 1 are taken in random order within each class. The strategy is
active only when the task's prior--data conflict exceeds $1/2$; otherwise it
shows label\_diversity's demonstration set (the same rows and order), so the
two strategies coincide exactly. It is related to misconfidence-based
selection \citep{xu2024misconfidence}, which prefers demonstrations on which
the model's output departs from the true label; here the model's own
zero-shot answer on each candidate plays that role. The design used the
surrogate's prediction; the measured prior replaced it after the surrogate
failed to fit (Section~\ref{sec:priors}). The code keeps the surrogate as a
fallback (\texttt{prior\_slopes}).

\paragraph{counter\_prior\_matched (exploratory, added during P4).} Qwen's
zero-shot answers rise with every value, including $f_8$'s, so where
$\sigma_t = -1$ the rows that contradict the prior are mostly rows on which
the shortcut is right: counter\_prior's sets held 91\% shortcut-agreeing rows
there, against 84\% for label\_diversity (\texttt{hiccups/16}). The matched
variant takes label\_diversity's set for the same seed as a reference and
keeps its number of rows in each (label, $f_8$ agrees) cell, so label counts
and the shortcut's apparent reliability match label\_diversity's exactly;
within each cell it prefers clean prior-contradicting rows, then other clean
rows, then label-noise rows. Where counter\_prior is inactive it is
label\_diversity's set. It is not part of the contrast family.

\subsection{Counter-spurious selection must match on \texorpdfstring{$|f_8|$}{|f8|}}

The goal is a demonstration set in which $f_8$ carries no information about
the label, so the model is not taught the shortcut. When demonstrations are
consistent with more than one feature, models show systematic feature
preferences \citep{si2023measuring} and exploit shortcuts in the prompt
\citep{tang2023large}, and in-context learners trained on tasks with
spurious features adopt them \citep{harutyunyan2026incontext,chen2026incontext}.
Demonstrations that break a spurious association improve generalisation
\citep{fan2024comparable}. Worst-group robustness is defined over
(label, spurious attribute) groups \citep{sagawa2020distributionally}, and
simply balancing those groups is a competitive remedy
\citep{idrissi2022simple}. Balancing the four
(label $\times$ agreement) cells is not enough, however.
Throughout this subsection $f_8$ is read in the task's direction,
$\sigma_t f_8$ (Section~\ref{sec:spurious}); $|f_8|$ and the agreement flags
do not depend on $\sigma_t$, so neither do the selected sets.

\begin{proposition}[Naive balancing leaves $f_8$ informative]
\label{prop:naive-balance}
Take equal numbers of rows from the four cells $(y, \text{agree})$. Then
sign agreement is exactly $1/2$, but
\[
  \Cov\!\big(f_8,\, 2y-1\big) = \tfrac12\Big(
  \underbrace{d + \tfrac{\phi(d)}{\Phi(d)}}_{\E[\,|f_8| \;\mid\; \mathrm{agree}\,]}
  \;-\;
  \underbrace{\tfrac{\phi(d)}{1 - \Phi(d)} - d}_{\E[\,|f_8| \;\mid\; \mathrm{disagree}\,]}
  \Big) > 0 .
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Within class $y=1$, $f_8 \sim \mathcal{N}(d,1)$. Agreeing rows are
$f_8 > 0$, with $\E[f_8 \mid f_8 > 0] = d + \phi(d)/\Phi(d)$; disagreeing
rows are $f_8 < 0$, with $\E[f_8 \mid f_8 < 0] = d - \phi(d)/(1-\Phi(d))$.
The product $f_8(2y-1)$ equals $+|f_8|$ on agreeing rows and $-|f_8|$ on
disagreeing rows; class $y=0$ is symmetric. With equal cell counts,
$\E[f_8] = 0$ and the covariance is the average of $f_8(2y-1)$, which gives
the expression. It is positive because agreeing rows lie further from zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{center}
{\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{5pt}
\begin{tabular}{ccccccc}
\toprule
$s$ & $d$ & sd$(f_8)$ & $\E|f_8|$ agree & $\E|f_8|$ disagree & naive $\corr(f_8, y)$ & zero-cov.\ counter share \\
\midrule
0.80 & 0.842 & 1.307 & 1.192 & 0.558 & 0.281 & 0.681 \\
0.85 & 1.036 & 1.440 & 1.311 & 0.518 & 0.334 & 0.717 \\
0.90 & 1.282 & 1.626 & 1.477 & 0.473 & 0.391 & 0.757 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\end{center}

A simulation at $s = 0.85$ gives $\corr = 0.332$ for naive balancing. The v2
rule ($k/2 + 1$ counter-spurious rows, a share of $0.625$ at $k=8$) gives
sign agreement $0.375$, reversing $f_8$'s sign relation and mirroring the
spurious-reversal test, while the covariance stays positive ($0.168$).

\begin{definition}[Magnitude-matched counter-spurious]
Within each class, draw $k/4$ disagreeing rows at random; for each, take
the agreeing row of the same class with the nearest $|f_8|$, without
replacement (nearest-neighbour matching on $|f_8|$;
\citealp{rubin1973matching,stuart2010matching}), with $|f_8|$ on the
generator scale. This requires $k$ divisible by 4, which holds for
$k \in \{8, 16, 32\}$. Label-noise rows ($y \neq y_{\mathrm{clean}}$) are
excluded on both sides: they are mostly disagreeing rows (a flipped label
makes an agreeing $f_8$ disagree), so leaving them in would give
counter-spurious sets several times the pool's noise rate. If a class has
fewer than $k/4$ eligible disagreeing rows, the remaining slots are filled
with random rows of that class and the shortfall is recorded.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
With perfect matching, sign agreement is $1/2$ and
$\Cov(f_8, 2y-1) = 0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Within each class, agreeing and disagreeing rows then have the same
distribution of $|f_8|$, so $f_8(2y-1)$, which is $+|f_8|$ or $-|f_8|$ with
equal frequency, has mean 0; $\E f_8 = 0$ by class symmetry.
\end{proof}

With a 256-row pool matching is close to perfect; the simulation gives
$\corr = 0.000$ and agreement $0.500$. The G1 gate checks that the
correlation, pooled over counter-spurious demonstration sets, is within
$\pm 0.1$ of zero. On the 24 evaluation tasks, pooled over $k = 8, 16, 32$,
the implementation gives $\corr(f_8, y) = 0.005$ and agreement exactly
$0.500$, with no shortfall.

\section{Seeding, reuse and pairing}
\label{sec:seeding}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1)}

Every random choice uses its own NumPy \citep{harris2020array} stream,
derived as
\[
  \texttt{SeedSequence([base, task, strategy, seed, purpose, ...])}
\]
(\texttt{demo\_seed} in \texttt{src/selection/protocols.py}), with 3 demo
seeds per task and strategy. Task and strategy names enter through CRC-32
hashes, so no seed depends on list order or on Python's per-process hash
salt. \texttt{purpose} separates the selection draw, the order shuffle and
the label shuffle of the P2 control; a query-conditional strategy also
passes the query's row id. The seed never includes the model, the naming,
the label mode or the environment, so those comparisons see the same
demonstration rows.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Query-agnostic strategies} draw one demonstration set per
        (task, strategy, seed). It is reused for every query of that task,
        in every environment, and under every naming for naming-blind
        strategies. counter\_prior depends on the model and the naming
        (through the measured prior), so it is reused across environments
        only; where it is inactive it shows label\_diversity's set.
  \item \textbf{Query-conditional strategies} (feature\_knn, SATA) select
        per query.
  \item \textbf{Order:} a seeded shuffle per demonstration set, so position
        is a controlled nuisance; order alone can swing in-context accuracy
        \citep{lu2022fantastically}.
  \item \textbf{Shuffled labels (P2):} the same rows in the same order, with
        their labels permuted within the set, which keeps the label counts
        and breaks the input--label mapping \citep{min2022rethinking}.
\end{itemize}

This gives three pairings used by the analysis: the same queries across
strategies; the same demonstration set across environments (the shift gap
of a query-agnostic strategy is a within-prompt contrast, free of
demonstration-sampling variance); and the same demonstration set across
namings for naming-blind strategies. v2 passed one fixed seed to every call,
so each strategy used a single demonstration set for all queries.

\section{Pool provenance and leakage}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1)}

Demonstrations always come from the task's ID pool. Covariate and
spurious-reversal queries are therefore answered with ID demonstrations, the
deployment-realistic setting; concept shift is the flipped naming on ID data
(Proposition~\ref{prop:concept-naming}).

\begin{proposition}[No test leakage]
No row of \texttt{test\_id} or of any \texttt{test\_ood} split reaches any
selector, standardisation statistic or prior estimate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Selectors receive only the pool; standardisation statistics are computed on
the pool; prior surrogates are fitted on synthetic random profiles, and

(Section~\ref{sec:priors}); SATA is trained on separate training tasks.
\end{proof}

v2 violated this: its counter-spurious proxy search used \texttt{test\_ood}
rows and labels.

\section{Demonstrations without the shortcut (exploratory, added 5 October 2026)}
\label{sec:demo-mask}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented and tested (\texttt{src/data/demo\_mask.py}); run pending
(stage E; notebook \texttt{03.1}); \texttt{hiccups/20}}

\paragraph{Motivation.} Demonstrations are examples meant to point the model
to the signal that causes the label. The user's argument is that they should
therefore not show the shortcut feature, or the noise feature. The P4 design
shows every feature because, in real data, no one knows which feature is
spurious. This experiment asks how much of the shortcut problem disappears
when the demonstrations never show the shortcut, while a query, as a new case
would, still does. It does not replace the P4 design, and it is not part of
the frozen contrast family.

\begin{definition}[Demo masks]
A mask $M \subseteq \{\text{spurious}, \text{noise}\}$ hides features from
every demonstration line. Three arms of the grid are run: \texttt{none}
($M = \emptyset$, full demonstrations), \texttt{spurious} (Part 1) and
\texttt{spurious\_noise} (Part 2). The hidden displayed features are
$f_{\pi_t(8)}$ for the shortcut and $f_{\pi_t(9)}$ for the noise feature: the
generator's columns 8 and 9 under the task's recorded column-role
permutation $\pi_t$ (Section~\ref{sec:roles}). They are looked up, never
estimated, and checked against the roles in the manifest.
\end{definition}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Queries are complete.} Every query and the content-free query
        show all ten features.
  \item \textbf{Selection is blind to hidden features.} The selection context
        holds the visible features only: feature\_range ranks them by mutual
        information, and similarity measures distance on them. counter\_prior
        reads Qwen's zero-shot prior re-measured on the masked pool rows
        (Section~\ref{sec:priors}).
  \item \textbf{Strategies that select on the shortcut} (counter\_spurious,
        counter\_prior\_matched) are refused under a mask that hides it.
        counter\_spurious runs in the full arm, where it neutralises the shortcut
        instead of removing it.
  \item \textbf{System message.} Masked demonstrations show 9 or 8 measurements
        and the query 10, so every arm uses the message without the count:
        ``Each example lists numeric measurements and a label (0 or 1).''
        The full arm is therefore rerun rather than taken from P4.
  \item \textbf{Scope.} Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, $k = 8$, the 24 evaluation
        tasks, three namings, three seeds (similarity one), and the id,
        covariate and spurious-reversal queries.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Same rows]
\label{prop:demo-mask-rows}
For random, label\_diversity and rule\_diversity, the demonstration rows and
their order are the same in every arm. The prompts of the three arms then
differ only in the hidden features' name--value pairs on the demonstration
lines and, against P4, in the system message's count.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Seeds never include the mask (Section~\ref{sec:seeding}). The random
mechanism reads no feature, and gt\_regime reads the regime, the label and
the clean label only, so the selected rows and the seeded order do not
depend on which features are visible. Queries are serialised from all ten
features in every arm.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Analysis} (\texttt{demo\_mask\_contrasts}). AUROC contrasts,
paired by task, seed and query, with the hierarchical bootstrap, for every
strategy, naming and environment:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item M1: no shortcut $-$ full;
  \item M2: no shortcut or noise $-$ full;
  \item M3: no shortcut or noise $-$ no shortcut (what removing the noise feature adds);
  \item M4: the change in the ID $-$ spurious-reversal gap, no shortcut $-$ full;
  \item M5: label\_diversity without the shortcut $-$ counter\_spurious with full
        demonstrations (removing against neutralising).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Expectations, stated before the run.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item If Qwen learns the shortcut from the demonstrations, hiding it lowers
        ID AUROC and raises spurious-reversal AUROC (M4 $< 0$).
  \item The query still shows the shortcut, and Qwen's zero-shot habit is
        ``bigger value, label 1'' (Section~\ref{sec:spurious}). A gap that
        remains, with opposite signs for $\sigma_t = \pm 1$, is shortcut use
        through the prior.
  \item The noise feature is unrelated to the label, so M3 should be close to 0.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Compute.} Stage E of \texttt{scripts/run\_p4\_pod.sh} on one H200,
with 8 MPS workers: pool priors for the three arms ($3 \times 24 \times 3
\times 256$ zero-shot rows), then the three grids. It is about 310{,}000 forward
passes. A one-task smoke run on the Mac rendered all three arms
through Qwen's chat template with label mass 1.0 and no prefix fallbacks.

\section{Scoring}
\label{sec:scoring}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1)}

Predictions are read from the probabilities of the label tokens rather than
from generated text, the usual way of scoring language models on
classification tasks \citep{brown2020language,zhao2021calibrate}.
For each prompt, \texttt{HFRunner}:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item tokenises the rendered prompt (no added special tokens);
  \item runs the decoder once (without the output layer), so the output
        layer is applied at the last position only;
  \item computes the logits of the tokens \texttt{0} and \texttt{1} in fp32
        from the final normed hidden state $h_L$ (two rows of the output
        layer), takes the full-vocabulary log-normaliser from the model's
        output layer at the last position with those two logits substituted
        in fp32 (so the label probabilities never sum to more than 1), and
        forms the two-way probability as a logistic of the label-logit
        difference, which cannot underflow.
\end{enumerate}
The model's own bf16 output layer rounds its logits to bf16, a step of
0.125 at logit sizes near 20. In the first P2 run this put every margin on
a 1/16 grid, left 19\% of the $p_1$ values within a demonstration set exactly
tied, and made cached and full passes disagree on near-ties (Llama: 96\% of
predictions, margin differences at most 0.25). Computing the two label
logits in fp32 removes that rounding; everything before the output layer is
unchanged. A test checks that the fp32 path equals the model's own output
when both run in fp32.
The runner (\texttt{src/inference/hf\_runner.py}) loads \texttt{.env}
before touching the Hugging Face hub, because the gated Llama repository
needs \texttt{HF\_TOKEN} even when every file is cached.

\begin{lemma}[Margin]
\label{lem:margin}
With $p_1 = e^{\ell_1}/(e^{\ell_0} + e^{\ell_1})$ (the constrained two-way
softmax) and $m = \ell_1 - \ell_0$, we have $p_1 = \sigmoid(m)$. The margin
is the same whether computed from logits or from full-vocabulary
log-probabilities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$p_1/(1-p_1) = e^{\ell_1 - \ell_0}$. Log-probabilities are logits minus a
common $\logsumexp$, which cancels in the difference.
\end{proof}

Prediction is $\hat y = \1[m > 0]$ and confidence is $\max(p_0, p_1)$.
Because scoring is constrained to the two label tokens, no prediction is
invalid.

\section{Prefix caching}
\label{sec:prefix-cache}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1)}

All queries that share a demonstration set share the prompt prefix up to
the query line. The prefix is run once; each query suffix is scored against
the cached keys and values, and the cache is cropped back to the prefix
length afterwards. Key--value caching is standard in transformer inference
\citep{pope2023efficiently}, and reusing the cache of a shared prompt prefix
is how serving systems avoid recomputing it
\citep{gim2024prompt,zheng2024sglang}; the proposition below states when the
reuse is exact.

\begin{proposition}[Exactness]
For a decoder-only transformer with causal (masked) attention
\citep{vaswani2017attention}, the hidden states,
and hence the cached keys and values, at positions $< |P|$ depend only on
the tokens at those positions. Running prefix $P$ once and then suffix $S$
against the cache yields the same final-position logits as a full forward
pass on $P \Vert S$, provided that $\text{tok}(P \Vert S) =
\text{tok}(P) \Vert \text{tok}(S)$ and the positions of $S$ continue from
$|P|$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By induction over layers. Embeddings, normalisation and MLPs act position
by position; attention at position $t$ reads keys and values at positions
$\leq t$ only. So the states at positions $< |P|$ are unchanged by
appending $S$, and the states at the positions of $S$ are computed from the
same inputs in both cases.
\end{proof}

Equality holds up to floating-point reduction order. The runner checks the
token-prefix condition for every suffix, because a byte-pair tokenizer
\citep{sennrich2016neural} can break it at the boundary; a suffix that fails
the check is scored with a full forward pass instead, and the runner counts
these fallbacks (none so far: prompts split at the start of the query line,
which the tokenizers treat as a word boundary). After each suffix the cache
is cropped back to the prefix length. Checks:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item G1 (passed): on the tiny test model, cached and full label
        log-probabilities agree within $10^{-4}$ (they are identical on CPU
        and MPS), and scoring the suffixes in reverse order gives the same
        values, so cropping leaves nothing behind;
  \item P2 (passed for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in bf16): caching moves the mean
        per-task AUROC by at most 0.005, a tenth of the learnability
        threshold; measured 0.0025, with 99.6\% prediction agreement. The
        plan's first rule, at least 99\% prediction agreement, was replaced
        because a prediction can flip only on a near-tie, so its rate
        depends on the model's margins rather than on the cache: all five
        Llama flips had $|m| \leq 0.022$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contextual calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P1)}

Following \citet{zhao2021calibrate}, the model's two-way output
$(c_0, c_1)$ on a content-free query (every value replaced by
\texttt{N/A}) with the same demonstration prefix estimates its bias for that
prompt. The calibrated probability is
\[
  \tilde p_1 = \frac{p_1 / c_1}{p_1/c_1 + p_0/c_0}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Calibration preserves ranks within a prompt]
\label{lem:calibration}
For fixed $(c_0, c_1)$, $\tilde p_1$ is strictly increasing in $p_1$. So
within one demonstration set calibration does not change the ranking of
queries, and AUROC computed within a demonstration set is unchanged; it
changes thresholded metrics and AUROC pooled across demonstration sets.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\tilde p_1 = \big(1 + \tfrac{1-p_1}{p_1}\cdot\tfrac{c_1}{c_0}\big)^{-1}$
and $(1-p_1)/p_1$ is strictly decreasing in $p_1$. AUROC depends on the
scores only through their ranking \citep{fawcett2006introduction}.
\end{proof}

Zero-shot predictions are reported uncalibrated: with no demonstrations, the
content-free prompt measures the prior this thesis studies.

\section{Measuring priors}
\label{sec:priors}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P3); G3 failed on the surrogate's fit, naming kept with measured priors}

\begin{definition}[Prior surrogate]
For a model and domain, the zero-shot margin $m_0(x; N)$ is measured on
random profiles $x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_{10})$ in z-units, rounded to 2
decimals as in every prompt. Each profile names 3 columns from 3 distinct
pairs (a random member of each) and the other 7 with distinct weak names, in
random positions, as in a named task prompt, so that name and position are
uncorrelated. Every model scores the same profiles. A ridge regression
\citep{hoerl1970ridge} with $\alpha = 1$ fits
\[
  m_0(x; N) \approx \beta_0 + \sum_{j} \beta_{N(j)}\, x_j ,
\]
giving each name a prior slope $\beta_{\mathrm{name}}$: its direction is
$\sign \beta$ and its strength $|\beta|$. Fit quality is the out-of-fold
$R^2$ (5 folds); slope intervals come from 500 bootstrap resamples of the
profiles. In the abstract condition the names are \texttt{f0}--\texttt{f9}
in their own positions.
\end{definition}
The surrogate is a global surrogate model of the LLM's zero-shot behaviour:
an interpretable model fitted to a black box's outputs on sampled inputs
\citep{craven1996extracting}. LIME \citep{ribeiro2016why} fits such models
locally.

\paragraph{Screening (rules fixed before the run).} 800 profiles per domain
cover every candidate name (8 pairs and 10 weak names per domain,
Appendix~\ref{app:lexicon}); 400 profiles cover the abstract names. A pair
$(p, q)$ is admitted if $\beta_p > 0 > \beta_q$, both 95\% intervals exclude
0, and $\min(|\beta_p|, |\beta_q|) / \max(|\beta_p|, |\beta_q|) \geq 1/2$; a
weak name is admitted if $|\beta|$ is at most a quarter of the median
$|\beta|$ of the admitted pair members. The code is
\texttt{scripts/p3\_prior\_screen.py}.

\paragraph{Result (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct).}

\midrule

loan & 0.20 & $-2.33$ & weak names $+0.29$ on average; 1 of 8 pairs has $\beta_q < 0$ with an interval below 0 \\
medical & 0.28 & $+1.41$ & weak names $+0.48$ on average; no negative slope below $-0.05$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item A numeric prior dominates: under any name, a larger value makes
        label 1 more likely. This is the prior found in P2
        (Section~\ref{sec:spurious}).
  \item Names that should lower $P(1)$ mostly weaken the slope instead of
        reversing it. Only \emph{missed payments} reverses clearly

        and \emph{HDL cholesterol} ($+0.35$) push towards high risk.
  \item The named fits are poor, and flexible models do not rescue them:
        gradient boosting over names, values and positions reaches 0.29

\end{itemize}

In loan every kept contrast has an interval above 0 (the smallest is 1.34,
$[0.66, 2.17]$). In medical only \emph{systolic blood pressure} /
\emph{weekly exercise hours} is clear (1.62, $[0.92, 2.32]$); the other
four lie between 0.29 and 0.67, and two intervals include 0.

\begin{definition}[Measured prior]
For task $t$, naming $N$ and pool row $i$, the measured prior
$\tilde m_0(x_i; N)$ is the model's zero-shot margin on that row, under the
same system message and names as the grid, minus its median over the pool.
\end{definition}
Centring removes the model's label bias (the intercept), as contextual
calibration and AUROC remove it elsewhere, so the prior predicts label 1 for
half of the pool. The measured prior is exact on the rows it is used on and
costs 256 zero-shot queries per (task, naming)
(\texttt{scripts/p3\_pool\_priors.py}, \texttt{PoolPrior}). It replaces the
surrogate in counter\_prior and in the conflict statistic below; the
surrogate is kept as a description of the name priors.

\begin{definition}[Prior--data conflict]
For task $t$ under naming $N$, with $\hat y_0(x) = \1[\tilde m_0(x; N) > 0]$,
\[
  C_t(N) = 1 - \frac{1}{|\text{pool}|} \sum_{i \in \text{pool}}
  \1[\hat y_0(x_i) = y_i].
\]
\end{definition}
Aligned tasks should have $C_t < 1/2$ and flipped tasks $C_t > 1/2$; this
is checked on the evaluation tasks once the pool priors are measured
(\texttt{results/v3/synthetic/p3/naming\_gate.json}). The name follows
prior--data conflict in Bayesian model checking \citep{evans2006checking}.
For language models, context that contradicts parametric knowledge is
studied as knowledge conflict \citep{longpre2021entity,xie2024adaptive};
here the conflict is between the rule the names imply and the rule the
demonstrations show.

\paragraph{Random-label control.} Demonstrations with shuffled labels keep
the label marginals and format but break the input--label mapping
\citep{min2022rethinking}, separating what the model takes from the mapping
from what it takes from format and prior. In P4 it is run for
label\_diversity under every naming.

\paragraph{Gate G3.} Pre-registered: out-of-fold $R^2 \geq 0.5$ in every
domain, and zero-shot AUROC under aligned minus flipped naming $\geq 0.15$ on
the pilot's ID queries (the names really carry priors). The draft stated the
second criterion in accuracy; AUROC was fixed before the check ran, because
zero-shot predictions are uncalibrated and raw accuracy mixes the model's
label bias into the contrast.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $R^2$: 0.20 (loan) and 0.28 (medical), so the criterion fails.
  \item Separation, on the 12 pilot tasks with the final names: zero-shot
        AUROC 0.499 abstract, 0.594 aligned and 0.430 flipped; the
        difference is 0.164, 95\% interval $[0.053, 0.287]$, positive on 10
        of 12 tasks (raw accuracy: 0.113). By domain it is 0.278 (loan) and
        0.050 (medical).
  \item Decision (the user's, 30 September 2026): keep the naming factor.
        counter\_prior and $C_t$ use the measured prior, so the surrogate's
        fit no longer matters for any result; medical is reported as a
        weak-prior domain.
\end{itemize}

\section{Design and inference}
%======================================================================
\status{Hierarchical bootstrap implemented (P2); contrasts and Holm correction in P4}

\paragraph{Units.} Task (the unit of generalisation over rules), query
within task, and demonstration seed. Per model, the full grid has 24 tasks
$\times$ 3 namings $\times$ 3 environments $\times$ 20 queries, crossed with
strategies and 3 seeds.

\paragraph{Why more tasks.} For a per-task effect with between-task
variance $\sigma_T^2$ and within-task variance $\sigma_W^2$, the variance of
the grand mean is $\sigma_T^2/T + \sigma_W^2/(Tn)$ (two-stage sampling;
\citealp{cochran1977sampling}), so tasks, not queries, control precision
when rules differ. v2 evaluated one task with $n = 30$
queries per cell (95\% interval half-width $0.179$ at accuracy $0.5$).

\paragraph{Hierarchical bootstrap.} For a contrast between conditions $A$
and $B$ on paired queries: (1) resample tasks with replacement; (2) within
each resampled task, resample queries with replacement; (3) compute the
metric difference on the resample; repeat $B = 2000$ times and report the
percentile interval \citep{efron1993introduction}
(\texttt{bootstrap\_ci}, \texttt{paired\_bootstrap\_diff} from
\texttt{main}). Resampling at both levels respects the nesting of queries
within tasks \citep{davison1997bootstrap,saravanan2020application}.

\paragraph{Per-task sign tests} count tasks on which $A$ beats $B$ (binomial
sign test; \citealp{dixon1946statistical}), guarding against a few tasks
driving the mean.

\paragraph{Multiplicity.} Holm correction \citep{holm1979simple} over a
pre-registered contrast family \citep{nosek2018preregistration}, frozen on
30 September 2026 before any P4 result existed (\texttt{CONTRASTS} in
\texttt{src/evaluation/analysis.py}). All ten contrasts are one-sided (the
direction below is the alternative), paired by task and query, and tested
with the hierarchical bootstrap (\texttt{hierarchical\_contrast}, $B = 2000$);
their p-values are Holm-adjusted together. The statistic for C1 and C2 is
AUROC, computed within a cell from the calibrated $p_1$ (for a query-agnostic
strategy this equals the AUROC of the raw $p_1$; for similarity it removes
each query's own prompt bias). Calibrated balanced accuracy is reported
alongside and is not part of the family. The family was first frozen with
calibrated balanced accuracy; on the same day, after the first 11 tasks
(seed 0, abstract names) showed that contextual calibration often leaves every
query of a plain-random cell on one side of the threshold (36\% of cells),
which pins balanced accuracy at 0.5 and forces those cells' shift gaps to 0,
the user chose AUROC, the study's primary metric and the metric of the P2
and G3 gates (\texttt{hiccups/15}).
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=C\arabic*.]
  \item RQ1, random demonstrations. (a) ID minus covariate and (b) ID minus
        spurious reversal, both under abstract names, $> 0$; (c) aligned
        minus flipped names on ID queries (concept shift) $> 0$.
  \item RQ2. (a) counter\_spurious minus label\_diversity under spurious
        reversal; (b) similarity (feature\_knn) and (c) feature\_range minus
        label\_diversity under covariate shift; (d) counter\_prior minus
        label\_diversity under flipped names on ID queries; (e) the
        interaction, (counter\_spurious minus label\_diversity) under spurious
        reversal minus the same under covariate shift. All $> 0$; (a)--(c)
        and (e) under abstract names.
  \item RQ3. (a) DFI under abstract minus flipped names $> 0$; (b)
        $\rho(\pi_{\mathrm{true}}, \pi_{\mathrm{behav}})$ under abstract
        minus flipped names $> 0$.
  \item RQ4 (a separate family, fixed before P5): SATA-v2 $-$ best
        hand-designed strategy $\geq 0$ on worst-environment calibrated BA;
        SATA-MI $-$ SATA-v2 $> 0$ and SATA-MI $-$ SATA-names $> 0$ under
        flipped naming.
\end{enumerate}
Two points were settled when the draft was frozen. Balanced strategies are
compared with label\_diversity (random, balanced), not with free random, so
each RQ2 contrast compares mechanisms at fixed label counts
(Proposition~\ref{prop:balanced}); plain random remains the RQ1 baseline.
For a query-agnostic strategy an environment gap is a within-prompt
contrast, and the bootstrap resamples the ID and OOD queries of each task
separately.

%======================================================================
\section{Faithfulness and reliance (RQ3)}
\label{sec:faithfulness}
%======================================================================
\status{Implemented (P4): \texttt{src/experiments/rq3.py},
\texttt{scripts/run\_rq3.py}; analysis in \texttt{src/evaluation/analysis.py}}

As in the project's literature review, the design follows STaDS
\citep{li2025stads}, which measures decision faithfulness as the rank
correlation between a model's self-reported feature ranking and its ranking
from leave-any-out ablation. Self-explanations can be unfaithful
\citep{turpin2023language,lanham2023measuring}, and LLM-reported feature
importance has previously been compared with occlusion and LIME
\citep{huang2023can}.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $\pi_{\mathrm{true}}$: ground-truth importance from the generator
        (positive for load-bearing features, zero otherwise;
        \texttt{true\_importance\_scores} from \texttt{main}), available
        because the data are synthetic \citep{liu2021synthetic}.
  \item $\pi_{\mathrm{behav}}$: the mean drop in the correct-label margin
        $(2y-1)(m - m_{\mathrm{cf}})$ when feature $j$ of the query is
        replaced by a donor row's value (hot-deck imputation,
        \citealp{andridge2010review}; \texttt{hot\_deck\_impute\_feature}:
        one of the 5 nearest pool rows on the other 9 columns, at random).
        The drop in calibrated accuracy is reported alongside; with 20
        queries it moves in steps of 0.05, and its ties make the rank
        correlation unstable, so the margin version is primary (fixed
        before P4).
        Drawing the replacement from the data keeps the perturbed query in
        distribution, the principle \citet{zhu2026faithfulness} argue for
        image attribution. It is removal by marginal sampling in the
        framework of \citet{covert2021explaining}, and resembles permutation
        importance \citep{breiman2001random}.
  \item $\pi_{\mathrm{self}}$: the model's own ranking. After the same
        demonstrations, the ranking prompt asks: ``Rank all 10 measurements
        from most to least important for predicting the label. Respond with
        the measurement names only, separated by commas, most important
        first.'' Its system message is the grid's without the answer-format
        sentence. The greedy answer is matched against the display names
        (\texttt{parse\_ranking}, which also accepts list markers and case
        changes); unlisted columns go last, and the number listed is
        recorded.
  \item Correctness $\rho(\pi_{\mathrm{true}}, \pi_{\mathrm{behav}})$ and
        internal consistency $\rho(\pi_{\mathrm{self}}, \pi_{\mathrm{behav}})$,
        Spearman with bootstrap intervals, by naming.
\end{itemize}

\begin{definition}[Directional sensitivity and demo-following index]
For query $x$ and load-bearing feature $j$, let
$g_j = m(x + \delta e_j) - m(x - \delta e_j)$ with $\delta = 0.5$ z-units.
The response follows the data if $\sign g_j = s_j$ and follows the name if
$\sign g_j$ equals the name's lexicon direction ($+1$ for a pair's first
member, $-1$ for its second). The demo-following index is the fraction of
(query, load-bearing feature) pairs with $\sign g_j = s_j$.
\end{definition}
The draft read the name's direction from the surrogate, $\sign \beta_{N(j)}$;
with Qwen's numeric prior almost every $\beta$ is positive
(Section~\ref{sec:priors}), so that version would test ``bigger values mean
1'' rather than the name, and the lexicon direction is used instead.

\paragraph{Implementation.} A unit is (model, naming, task, seed). It shows
the grid's label\_diversity demonstration set for that task and seed (the
same rows and order) and scores, against one cached prefix, every ID query,
each query with each of its 10 columns replaced by its donor value, each
query with each load-bearing column and $f_8$ moved by $\pm 0.5$ (so reliance
on the shortcut is also read, in the task's direction $\sigma_t$), and the
content-free query; 381 suffixes per unit. Donors depend only on the task
and the column, so every naming and seed sees the same perturbed queries.
The ranking prompt follows. Seed 0 runs first.
Under flipped naming, following the data and following the name are
mutually exclusive, so DFI measures, feature by feature, which side of the
contest wins. It plays the role, for individual features, of the
flipped-label accuracy of \citet{wei2023larger}.

%======================================================================
\section{Architecture}
%======================================================================
\status{Planned (P5): ported from main}

SATA was proposed in the project's literature review. It draws on
In-Context Risk Minimization \citep{gupta2024context}, in which a
transformer reads other inputs from the test environment as context in
order to adapt to that environment.

SATA scores each candidate demonstration given the whole pool and the
query. Candidates are embedded as feature vector plus label embedding plus a
demonstration-type embedding; the query is embedded as its feature vector
plus a query-type embedding; a transformer encoder ($d = 128$, 4 heads,
4 layers) attends over all tokens; a linear head gives one score per
candidate, normalised by a softmax. The query-agnostic ablation replaces the
query with a zero vector. Selection takes the balanced top-$k$.

The row encoding resembles TabPFN \citep{hollmann2023tabpfn}, which also
embeds each labelled row as one token and lets query rows attend to them.
There are no positional encodings, so the encoder is permutation
equivariant over candidates, the property that set-based attention models
are built around \citep{lee2019set}.

\paragraph{Relation to learned selectors.} Training a demonstration
selector from language-model feedback is established: EPR
\citep{rubin2022learning} and UDR \citep{li2023unified} train retrievers on
the model's scores of candidate demonstrations, CEIL
\citep{ye2023compositional} learns to select whole sets, and
\citet{zhang2022active} learn a selection policy by reinforcement learning.
Signals from model internals and learned judges of demonstration sets have
also been proposed \citep{kato2025affinity,wang2026disp}. SATA differs in
being trained on synthetic tasks with ground-truth structural targets, in
targeting robustness to controlled shift, and, in the MI variants, in using
the model's measured priors.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Variant} & \textbf{Per-row input} & \textbf{Width} \\
\midrule
SATA-v2 & $x$ (z-scored, displayed order) & 10 \\
SATA-names (control) & $[x,\ s^{\text{lex}} \odot x]$, lexicon directions $\pm 1$, 0 for neutral names & 20 \\
SATA-MI & $[x,\ \beta \odot x,\ \hat m_0(x)]$, measured prior slopes and surrogate margin & 21 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Since P3 the surrogate is descriptive only (its fit failed G3,
Section~\ref{sec:priors}); P5 may take $\hat m_0$ from the measured prior on
the pool rows and the zero-shot margin of the query instead.

%======================================================================
\section{Training targets}
%======================================================================
\status{Planned (P5)}

\paragraph{Structural targets (SATA-v2, as on main).}
For candidate $i$ and query $q$,
\[
  \tau_i = 2\,\1[r_i = r_q] + 1.5\,\1[\text{counter}_i]
          - 1.5\,\1[\text{consistent}_i \wedge r_i \neq r_q],
  \qquad
  q_i = \operatorname{softmax}(\tau / T)_i ,
\]
where $r$ is the regime and counter/consistent refer to \texttt{agree\_obs}.
Softening the targets with a temperature $T$ follows knowledge distillation
\citep{hinton2015distilling}.

\begin{lemma}[No query-label term]
Every term of $\tau$ is a function of the candidate's features and label
and of the query's features. None depends on the query's label.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The regime is a function of features; agreement is a function of the
candidate's $f_8$ and label.
\end{proof}
(v1's targets rewarded candidates whose label matched the query's label,
which, with a near-copy $f_8$, taught SATA to read the query label from
$f_8$.)

\paragraph{SATA-MI.} Adds $\lambda\,\1[\text{counter-prior}_i \wedge
y_i = y_{\mathrm{clean},i}]$ when $C_t(N) > 1/2$, rewarding candidates that
contradict the prior and follow the rule when the prior is wrong.

\paragraph{SATA-MI-mech (datamodel utilities).} For a training task and
naming, sample $M = 128$ balanced subsets $S_1, \ldots, S_M$ of size $k$
from a 64-row candidate pool and measure, with the LLM,
\[
  U(S) = \frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_{q \in Q} (2y_q - 1)\, m(q; S),
\]
the mean margin toward the correct label over a label-balanced query set
$Q$ drawn from the training task. Fit
$U(S) \approx \theta_0 + \sum_i \theta_i \1[i \in S]$ by ridge regression,
a linear datamodel \citep{ilyas2022datamodels}, and use
$q = \operatorname{softmax}(\theta/T)$ as targets for the query-agnostic
head. The closest precedent is \citet{chang2023data}, who fit linear
datamodels to the correct-class margin of in-context examples to find
examples that stabilise in-context learning; \citet{nguyen2023incontext}
estimate similar influences by comparing random subsets with and without
each example. Here the fitted utilities become training targets for a
selector.

\begin{lemma}[No query-label leak]
$\theta$ is a property of the candidate pool: it depends on query labels
only through an average over a label-balanced set $Q$ from a training task.
Evaluation queries never enter any target, so SATA cannot learn to infer an
evaluation query's label.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Targets are computed on training tasks only, and the query-agnostic head
receives no query input.
\end{proof}

\section{Residual stream and logit lens}
%======================================================================
\status{Planned (P6)}

The logit lens \citep{nostalgebraist2020logit} reads a model's intermediate
predictions by applying the final normalisation and unembedding to each
layer's residual-stream state \citep{elhage2021mathematical}.
A forward pass with \texttt{output\_hidden\_states=True} returns $L+1$
tensors: the embeddings and the output of each layer. In transformers 5.17
the last one has already been through the final norm (verified: applying
\texttt{lm\_head} to it reproduces the logits, applying the norm again does
not). Let $h_l$ be the last-position state and
$w = W_U[\texttt{1}] - W_U[\texttt{0}]$ the difference of the two label rows
of the (untied) unembedding.

\begin{definition}[Logit-lens margin]
$m_l = w^\top \RMSNorm(h_l)$ for $l < L$, and $m_L = w^\top h_L$, where
$\RMSNorm$ is the model's final RMS normalisation layer
\citep{zhang2019root}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Final-layer identity]
$m_L$ equals the runner margin $m$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$h_L$ is the normed final state and $\ell = W_U h_L$, so
$m_L = \ell_1 - \ell_0 = m$.
\end{proof}
The P6 gate checks this identity within $10^{-3}$ in bf16. It is attainable
because the runner computes the label logits in fp32 from $h_L$, as the logit
lens does (Section~\ref{sec:scoring}); margins read from the bf16 output layer
would miss it by up to half a bf16 step, about 0.06.

%======================================================================
\section{Critical layer, probes and steering}
%======================================================================
\status{Planned (P6)}

\begin{definition}[Critical layer]
Let $\mathrm{acc}_l(N)$ be the accuracy of $\1[m_l > 0]$ against the rule
label with demonstrations under naming $N$. The critical layer of a task
is the smallest $l$ at which $\mathrm{acc}_l(\text{aligned}) -
\mathrm{acc}_l(\text{flipped})$ exceeds $0.1$ with a bootstrap interval
excluding zero: the point where prior and demonstration evidence diverge
\citep[after][]{halawi2024overthinking}.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Linear probes.} Per layer, logistic regressions on $h_l$ decode
the rule label and the prior prediction $\hat y^{\,0}$; the decodability
curves show where each kind of information is linearly available
\citep{alain2017understanding}. Probes complement the logit lens, whose
readings are biased across layers and unreliable for some model families
\citep{belrose2023eliciting}. Decodability
shows that information is present, not that the model uses it
\citep{belinkov2022probing}, which is why steering serves as the causal
check.

\paragraph{Steering (causal check).} On training tasks, take the difference
of mean states between prompts whose prediction follows the demonstrations
and prompts whose prediction follows the prior:
\[
  v_l = \bar h_l(\text{follows demonstrations}) - \bar h_l(\text{follows prior}).
\]
At the critical layer, set $h_l \leftarrow h_l + \alpha v_l$ with a forward
hook and measure the change in accuracy on held-out tasks. Steering is an
upper bound and a causal test of the diagnosis, not a selection method.
Difference-of-means steering vectors follow activation addition
\citep{turner2023activation} and contrastive activation addition
\citep{rimsky2024steering}. \citet{minder2025controllable} find a
context-versus-prior direction of this kind in Llama-3.1-8B, and task and
function vectors \citep{hendel2023incontext,todd2024function} are the
in-context-learning counterparts. Similar tools have been used to diagnose
false demonstrations \citep{halawi2024overthinking}.

\paragraph{Question for selection.} Do counter\_prior or SATA-MI
demonstrations move the divergence point, or raise $m_l$ toward the
demonstrated label earlier, relative to random demonstrations?
\citet{kato2025affinity} show that scores computed from induction-head
representations track in-context accuracy, and leave selection with such
scores to future work.

\section{Bug-hunting notes (v3 invariants to test)}

Items marked (tested) have a test in \texttt{tests/}; the rest arrive with
the phase that needs them.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Single-task pools.} Every demonstration id belongs to the
        query's task (tested, G1).
  \item \textbf{No test rows upstream.} Selectors, standardisation and
        priors never see \texttt{test\_id} or \texttt{test\_ood} rows (tested for selectors and standardisation; priors in P3).
  \item \textbf{$f_8$ is present in every environment.} Only spurious
        reversal tests reliance on it by flipping its relation to the label.
  \item \textbf{$f_8$ statistics on the generator scale.} Sign agreement
        changes under centring (Lemma~\ref{lem:standardise}); correlations
        do not.
  \item \textbf{Label noise comes after $f_8$.} About 2\% of rows have $f_8$
        misaligned with the observed label; counter-spurious selection uses
        the observed label, and both ground-truth strategies exclude
        label-noise rows from their candidates.
  \item \textbf{Pairing.} Demonstration seeds exclude the model, naming,
        label mode and environment, so gold and shuffled units show the same
        rows (tested with the grid engine).
  \item \textbf{Mechanism is not evaluated.} It is a placebo for in-context
        learning (Proposition~\ref{prop:mechanism}); it appears only in
        SATA training, with matched pools.
  \item \textbf{Aligned and flipped prompts differ only in three names.}
        Snapshot-test the system message and the non-load-bearing names.
  \item \textbf{Balanced composition.} Structured strategies always show
        $k/2$ labels of each class (tested).
  \item \textbf{Counter-spurious decorrelation.} Pooled
        $|\corr(\sigma_t f_8, y)| \leq 0.1$ in counter-spurious sets; naive cell
        balancing would give about $0.33$ (tested).
  \item \textbf{Covariate shift moves the rule's inputs.} Two
        load-bearing features in opposite label directions and one
        distractor, each by 1--2 standard deviations, with an unchanged
        label rate (tested). Report which features moved; do not filter
        shifts on the label rate.
  \item \textbf{Spurious direction.} $\sigma_t$ is balanced within each
        (family, domain) cell, and $\sigma_t = -1$ changes only the sign of
        $f_8$ (tested). Statistics of $f_8$ reliance read $f_8$ in the
        task's direction, and prior-aligned and prior-opposed tasks are
        reported separately as well as pooled.
  \item \textbf{Tokenisation.} One BOS on Llama; label tokens
        single-piece; base-model queries end in \texttt{"-> "} (tested, with the real Qwen and Llama tokenizers).
  \item \textbf{Prefix caching.} Check the token-prefix condition for every
        suffix and fall back to a full pass; compare cached and full scoring
        (tested on the tiny model; P2 on Qwen).
  \item \textbf{Constrained softmax.} $p_0, p_1$ renormalise over the two
        label tokens only; a model confident in some other token can still
        produce $p_1 \approx 0.5$. This is by design. Mass on other surface
        forms of the same answer (e.g.\ \texttt{" 1"}) is ignored, a form of
        surface-form competition \citep{holtzman2021surface}.
  \item \textbf{Output precision.} Compute the two label logits in fp32 from
        the final hidden state; the bf16 output layer quantises margins to a
        1/16 grid (tested: the fp32 path equals the model's own output in
        fp32).
  \item \textbf{Logit lens.} Do not apply the final norm to the last hidden
        state; it is already normed.
  \item \textbf{Feature order.} Frozen per task after $\pi_t$; ten columns
        with single-digit raw names, so alphabetical order equals displayed
        order. Column roles are read from the manifest (tested).
  \item \textbf{One device per comparison.} Results record device and dtype;
        never mix devices within a contrast, because kernels differ slightly
        across CUDA, MPS and CPU.
\end{enumerate}
