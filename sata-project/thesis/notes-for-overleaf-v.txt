C5. Chapter 6 probes: the sentence "The probes reuse the demonstration sets and prompts ... for
    zero-shot, random, ..., \sata and \satami" is wrong for the probes (random only) and still
    lists SATA-MI. Suggested fuller Probes paragraph:
      A linear probe \citep{alain2017understanding} is a logistic regression that takes $h_l$ as its input and predicts a label. Each prompt with random demonstrations gives one example, $h_l$ at its last token, so each naming has 1,440 (24 tasks, three environments, 20 queries). At every second layer and for each naming, two probes are trained, one per target: the query's rule label, and Qwen's zero-shot prediction for the same query (its zero-shot margin above or below the task's median). Each probe standardises its inputs and is L2-regularised ($C = 0.1$), since $h_l$ has 3,584 dimensions; it is trained on 18 tasks and scored by \AUROC on the other six, in four folds, so that every task is tested by a probe that never saw it.

C6. Chapter 6 steering paragraph: plainer version (also says what "zero-shot prediction" means):
      The lens and the probes only observe; steering intervenes. We take the queries on which the rule label and Qwen's zero-shot prediction (its zero-shot margin above or below the task's median) disagree, so that following the demonstrations and following the prior give different answers. At layer $l$, $v_l$ is the mean residual state of the queries on which Qwen, given random demonstrations, followed the demonstrations, minus the mean of those on which it followed its prior. It is computed separately for the two rule labels and the two differences are averaged, so that $v_l$ does not simply push towards one label \citep{turner2023activation,rimsky2024steering}. We fit $v_l$ on the 12 loan tasks and test it on the 12 medical tasks, adding $\alpha v_l$ to the residual state at the query's tokens only ($h_l \leftarrow h_l + \alpha v_l$), at layers 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24 and with $\alpha$ from 1 to 32. A random direction of the same length is the control: if it moves the answers as much, the effect is disruption, not steering.

G. DISCUSSION 7.3: THE "STEERING FAILING" CRITIQUE (local discussion.tex line 63)
=====================================================================

Why: within-cell AUROC cannot see a push that moves every margin together, and the steering vector
is exactly such a push, so a reader may suspect the metric hid an effect. It did not: the margins
are reported (Figure 6.x bottom), and accuracy at the model's own threshold also stays at chance.
Frame it as a check that closes the blind spot, not as a concession. The user's three sentences
are unchanged; G1 appends two.

G1. Suggested reply:

    \critique{Steering failing proves nothing about steering in general.}
    {Agreed. We tested one family of directions (a label-averaged mean difference at five layers, applied to the query) on one 7B model with eight numeric demonstrations, within a small budget. We claim only that this direction acts as a bias at this scale, and we explain why it must: following the demonstrations means opposite things for the two labels.
